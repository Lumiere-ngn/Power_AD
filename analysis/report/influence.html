Overview

Which telemetry features help predict which, after each device's own daily cycle has been removed. A link X → Y means that X's last two hours improve the forecast of Y beyond Y's own history and every other feature's history, on most devices.

    Influence graph

    Arrows are conditional Granger links; thickness is the share of devices where the link is significant. Colour is the sign of the lead–lag correlation (blue: they move together, red: opposite). Dashed arrows rest on fewer than 10 devices. Hover a feature to isolate its links, or an arrow for details.

    Edge strength

    Every ordered pair, including those below the drawing threshold: the share of tested devices where X Granger-causes Y after Benjamini–Hochberg correction (q = 0.01).

    Lead–lag

    Cross-correlation of the deseasonalized series at shifts of −12 to +12 hours, median across devices. A peak at a positive lag means the first feature moves first. Most physical links peak at 0–1 h: at hourly resolution, cause and effect usually land in the same or the next hour.

    Raw vs deseasonalized

    The same Granger test on raw hourly values finds many more links, because features that both follow the sun predict each other without influencing each other. These are the links that removing the daily cycle takes away.
