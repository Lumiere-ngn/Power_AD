Overview

Two questions on the hourly table of the fleet: which hours are unusual for a sign, and which variables lead which. Every score is made online, in time order: a model sees an hour only after it has scored it.

    Flagged hours

    Each sign and metric has its own Holt-Winters forecaster with a 24-hour cycle. The gap between the value and the forecast is divided by a running scale for that hour of day, which gives z. An hour is flagged as a spike when |z| ≥ 4, and as a sustained deviation when |z| ≥ 3 for three hours in a row. A flag is shared when enough peer signs flag the same metric in the same hour, and device-specific otherwise.

    Incidents

    An incident is a run of flagged hours on one sign, over all metrics, with breaks of at most two hours. It is counted as shared when at least half of its flagged hours are shared.

    Largest events

    The largest deviation of each sign and metric, ranked by |z|, with 36 hours on each side. The blue line is the measured value and the dashed line is the forecast made one hour earlier.

    Joint score over all metrics

    A second detector (River's Half-Space Trees) scores each sign-hour from the z values of all its metrics together. It looks for hours where the combination is unusual, even if no single metric crosses its own threshold.

    Causal graph

    An arrow from A to B means: on most signs, the last two hours of A improve the forecast of B's next hour, beyond what the last two hours of every other variable already give (conditional Granger causality, tested online). The variables are the z values above, so a link is between deviations from each variable's own daily cycle.

    Reading the links

      Link evidence

      A link is kept when at least half of the signs that could test it find it significant (false-discovery rate 1 % over all signs and pairs) and the median forecast gain is at least 0.5 % of the error. Links were found on 8 March to 30 June and then checked on July.

      Onset order inside incidents

      A second, independent look at direction: in incidents that flag both variables of a link, which one flagged first? If the link is real and slower than an hour, the cause should flag first more often than the effect.
